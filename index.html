MSEd TESOL Teaching Philosophy

Empowering Learners Through Task-Based Authenticity and Reflective Practice

My instructional philosophy is anchored in the principles of Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) and Outcome-Based Education (OBE). I believe language and academic skills are acquired most effectively through active, meaningful engagement with communicative challenges that reflect real-world demands.

01. Pedagogy

Student-Centered, Task-Based Learning

Drawing from Second Language Acquisition (SLA) research, I design instructional sequences moving from pre-task preparation to task execution in collaborative groups, followed by post-task focus on form. Decoupling complex communicative tasks into manageable sub-goals allows learners to build both procedural automaticity and confidence as active agents in their own skill development.

02. Inclusion & Equity

Inclusive & Diverse Learning Environments

Having taught over a decade in multicultural educational contexts, I prioritize creating environments that value diverse perspectives, accommodate varied learning styles, and support English as an Additional Language (EAL) learners. Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles guide my course design through multimodal delivery—incorporating visual assets, digital learning objects, and structured rubrics.

03. Assessment

Outcome-Based Assessment & Continuous Feedback

Assessment serves as a transparent roadmap for growth rather than a mere grading exercise. I align all activities and evaluation tools directly with course learning outcomes and Essential Employability Skills (EES). Through multi-tiered analytic rubrics, timely formative feedback, and structured self-reflection, students take ownership of their academic trajectory.

04. Innovation

Educational Technology & Pedagogical Innovation

I leverage Learning Management Systems (Canvas/Blackboard) and interactive digital tools (such as H5P, digital learning logs, and multimedia resources) to extend learning beyond the physical classroom. Technology in my courses is intentionally selected to enhance student interaction, facilitate collaborative problem-solving, and prepare students for tech-mediated academic environments.

Interactive Showcase

Response-Reactive Diagnostic & Cohort Grouping Engine

An interactive H5P module that evaluates learner proficiency in real time, routes students through dynamic diagnostic pathways, and algorithmically outputs balanced cohorts for collaborative group activities.

Live Interactive Demonstration Sandbox Active

1. Response-Reactive Branching

Uses multi-branch decision trees to dynamically adapt task difficulty based on real-time learner inputs, reducing test anxiety while capturing granular proficiency markers.

2. Hierarchical Scoring

Evaluates task performance across discrete linguistic and communicative metrics rather than simple pass/fail outcomes, generating multi-dimensional skill profiles.

3. Cohort Balancing Algorithm

Automatically pairs and groups learners into heterogenous or homogenous cohorts optimized for peer scaffolding during subsequent communicative tasks.

Scholarly Research Agenda

Bridging SLA Research, Task-Based Design, and EdTech

My research investigates how technology-mediated learning environments and structured task frameworks reduce cognitive load, promote grammatical fluency, and foster authentic communicative interaction in post-secondary contexts.

Inductive Consciousness-Raising

Investigating how Socratic questioning techniques help learners inductively discover grammatical patterns rather than memorizing explicit rules, supporting implicit-to-explicit knowledge transfer.

Performance-Based Fluency

Examining the impact of reflective self-monitoring and shadowing routines on L2 speech production, speech segmentation, and procedural automaticity during communicative tasks.

Algorithmic Cohort Grouping

Developing response-reactive diagnostic tools that evaluate real-time proficiency and algorithmically balance learner cohorts to optimize peer scaffolding (ZPD) during TBLT tasks.
